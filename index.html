Temperature is a dial for surprise, not knowledge

Turning it up makes the model braver about picking words it already ranked low. It doesn't give the model new words to pick from. The raw scores (logits) never change; temperature only rescales them: softmax(logit / T). At T → 0 the top word wins every time. Around 1 you get the model's own distribution. Above 1 the tail fattens. Output gets more surprising, and past about 1.5 it usually stops making sense.

Top-k vs top-p

Top-k ignores shape: set k to 5 and it keeps exactly 5 words, whether the model is sure or guessing. Top-p adapts: a confident model at p = 0.9 might keep 2 words; an uncertain one, 12. Set temperature high and top-p tight and you get variety without nonsense.

How they combine here

Order in this demo: temperature → top-k → top-p → renormalise the survivors → sample. Real inference stacks vary the order and add other filters (min-p, repetition penalties), but the intuition carries over.

Made to illustrate an idea, not a specific model. The candidate scores are hand-picked to look like a plausible output distribution. Real models sample tokens, which are often fragments of words rather than whole ones; the page says "word" for readability.
